Keep this as `project-profile.md` in the class's working folder, or paste it into the claude.ai
project's knowledge. The lesson-planning skill reads it first so it doesn't have to ask about any
of it again, and so every lesson is built for this room from the first draft. Describe students by
need, never by name. Leave out anything that doesn't apply; a short profile is still worth having.
Beside it the skill keeps the class notes on its own, in a file named exactly what the project is
called: what works with this class, the words they own, the pairings that work.

## Accommodations checklist

Tick what applies to students in this class. The skill builds each ticked item into every packet and
deck by default and says where it landed in the plan's Differentiation line, so the supports are
there before the lesson is offered, not added after.

- [ ] Sentence stems on every written answer
- [ ] Word bank above every question that needs vocabulary
- [ ] Read-aloud: every question and direction is on a slide, so it can be read to the room
- [ ] Reduced item count: core questions marked; the rest are (b) parts
- [ ] Large print packet (about 15 pt body type)
- [ ] Chunked directions: one step per line, numbered
- [ ] Visual support on every question (photo, diagram, or chart on the matching slide)

## Universal Design (UDL)

Design for the whole room, not supports for one student (those are the accommodations above).
Tick what this class needs most. Every lesson builds in at least one ticked item from each of the
three principles, and the plan's Differentiation line names which.

Engagement: why it matters to them
- [ ] A hook from our own project, place, or community in the do-now or the opening
- [ ] A real choice on at least one task: two contexts, or two ways to show it
- [ ] A game round or a vote that puts every student's guess in play
- [ ] A self-check or reflection line on the packet

Representation: how the idea reaches them
- [ ] Every key word highlighted where each section first uses it, pictured on a slide, said aloud,
      with its language line
- [ ] Every number with a physical referent shown as a photo or a chart
- [ ] A worked example on the slide before anyone works alone
- [ ] The same idea in two forms: a picture and a sentence, or a table and a chart

Action and expression: how they show it
- [ ] An organizer matched to the thinking (compare, sequence, argue, own a word)
- [ ] Choice of response: circle, point, say, or draw where the task allows
- [ ] Say it to a partner before writing it or reporting it
- [ ] Draw or build it before writing it
